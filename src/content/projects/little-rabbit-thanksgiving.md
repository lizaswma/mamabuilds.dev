## Why I built this

With Thanksgiving coming up, I wanted to teach my toddler what the holiday is really about:
being thankful for the people around you. "谢谢" (thank you) is already something she says, so
this book gives her a way to practice it, and to see that saying thank you is about something
specific someone does for you.

The rule in the story is simple: *say thank you, add a leaf.* Mama Rabbit hangs a bare paper
"thankful tree" on the wall, and every time Little Rabbit thanks someone, a new leaf goes up.
Then the family turns it around and thanks Little Rabbit, adding a big golden leaf at the top.
That twist is the part I care most about: kindness comes back to you.

I brought back familiar characters from the earlier books: Mama, Papa, Grandma, and Grandpa
Rabbit from [Little Rabbit's Mid-Autumn Festival](/projects/little-rabbit-mooncakes), and Mr. Cat
and Ms. Bear from [Little Rabbit's Halloween](/projects/little-rabbit-halloween). My daughter
already knows them, so the story feels like a visit with friends rather than meeting strangers.

## What it does

- Little Rabbit thanks six people for one thing each: Mama for reading to her, Papa for biking
  with her, Grandma for yummy food, Grandpa for gardening with her, Mr. Cat for being her friend,
  and Ms. Bear for taking her to the park. Each person holds the thing they're being thanked for,
  so a toddler who can't read can still see *why*.
- Teaches colors as well as counting. The first two books stuck to numbers; here every thank-you
  adds a leaf in a new color (red, orange, yellow, green, brown, purple, then gold), and the
  narration names the color and the running count together, counting up to seven.
- A counting page where she taps each leaf on the tree to light it up and count it, followed by a
  big Thanksgiving dinner and a good-night page.
- Full bilingual narration and text, with the same Mandarin/English toggle as the earlier books,
  plus a tap interaction on every page.
- Installable straight to an iPad home screen and works fully offline after the first load.

## How I built it

The personal part of this book is who gets thanked, and for what. I mapped each character to
something that person really does with my toddler. She loves gardening with her grandpa, so
Grandpa Rabbit is thanked for gardening with her. Her wonderful nanny takes her to the park all
the time, so Ms. Bear is thanked for taking Little Rabbit to the park. Each thank-you is
something she'll recognize from her own week.

Claude deserves the credit for most of the craft. It suggested giving every thank-you page the
same layout (tree on the left, Little Rabbit in the middle, the person she's thanking on the
right), so the pattern stays predictable and only the person changes. It also suggested breaking
that layout on the golden-leaf page, where everyone appears together for the first time as a cue
that something is different. And it carried the leaves over from page to page, the way the
stickers carried over in the Halloween book, so the tree fills up as the story goes and the
leaves she counts at the end are the ones she watched go up. Because the series' style bible
already existed, the only new art Little Rabbit needed was a cozy autumn scarf.
